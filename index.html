Frequently Asked Questions

Does this app need the internet or an AI subscription to work?

No. Every transcription runs locally in your browser using a built-in pronunciation dictionary and letter-to-sound rules - there's no API key, account, or network call involved once the page has loaded.

How accurate is the IPA transcription?

Very high for real English words: pronunciations come from a ~124,000-word dictionary derived from the CMU Pronouncing Dictionary. For words it doesn't recognize (rare names, invented words), it falls back to rule-based spelling-to-sound estimates, which are flagged with a ≈ so you always know when a result is a best guess rather than a verified pronunciation.

What's the difference between Broad and Narrow notation?

Broad (phonemic) notation, shown in slashes like /kæt/, captures the distinct sounds of a word. Narrow (phonetic) notation, shown in brackets like [kʰæt], adds finer detail such as aspiration and flapping for General American English.

Which accents are supported?

General American, British (RP), and Australian English. American is the dictionary's native accent; British and Australian are generated from it using standard phonological rules (like non-rhotic vowel mergers), so treat them as close approximations rather than a native RP/AuE dictionary.

Is my text or voice recording sent anywhere?

No. Text-to-IPA transcription and audio playback happen entirely on your device via your browser's built-in speech engine. Speech-to-IPA uses your browser's built-in speech recognition to get a transcript, which is then transcribed locally the same way as typed text.

Why does a phoneme sound like a whole word instead of just the symbol?

Speech synthesis engines can't reliably pronounce an isolated IPA character on its own. Instead, each symbol plays a real example word (like "cat" for /k/) so you hear the sound the way it actually occurs in speech.

Pronunciation data derived from the CMU Pronouncing Dictionary (public domain, Carnegie Mellon University). Unknown words are estimated with rule-based letter-to-sound heuristics and marked with ≈.
